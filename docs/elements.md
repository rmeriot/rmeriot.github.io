<h2 id="first-author-papers">First author papers</h2>


<p> <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260814423M/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> <strong> Lorelight : a star-formation-parameters agnostic field-level 21-cm emulator </strong></a>  <br> <em>Meriot et al 2026b. </em> <br> We present Lorelight, a transformer+Unet network that takes as input light cones of the density field and galaxy luminosities, and predicts lightcones of gas temperatures and ionisation.  </p>


<p> <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260814423M/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> <strong> How X-rays heat the IGM in different 21-cm simulation codes: a comparison between Licorice and Beorn </strong></a>  <br> <em>Meriot et al 2026a. </em> <br>  We compare the 3D radiative transfer of Licorice with the 1D scheme of Beorn, in the X-ray band, and investifate how physical approximations affect the heating of the IGM.  </p>


<p> <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A%26A...698A..80M/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> <strong> Comparison of Bayesian inference methods using the LORELI II
database of hydro-radiative simulations of the 21-cm signal </strong></a>  <br> <em>Meriot et al 2025.</em> <br> In this paper we present Loreli II, a database of 10 000 Licorice simulations of the 21-cm signal. We also compare three inference methods: MCMC with an emulator, and two flavours of Simulation-based Inference. </p>

<p> <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A%26A...683A..24M/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> <strong> The LORELI database: 21 cm signal inference with 3D radiative hydrodynamics simulations </strong></a>  <br> <em>Meriot et al 2024.</em> <br> We modify the Licorice simulation codes to implement two subgrid models affecting star formation and heating. We run a small database of a few hundred low-res simulations, Loreli I, and use it as a training sample for an emulator of the 21-cm power spectrum. </p>

<p> <a href="https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.512...27M/abstract" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> <strong> The Cosmic Mach Number as an environment measure for the underlying dark matter density field </strong></a>  <br> <em>Meriot et al 2022.</em> </p>
